20.4. Milestone 5 - Project Presentation (Team)#

At a Glance

What you will do: Present your team’s greenhouse control solution to Mission Control: the problem, your design, the evidence that shows it works, and what you learned.

What you will submit: Your slides and your team presentation, delivered live or recorded as your instructor directs.

Your report is the written record of the project. Your presentation delivers that work to the client. Base every claim on evidence. A busy audience should be able to follow it in a few minutes.

20.4.1. Presentation Outline#

Plan for the time your instructor sets. Cover the five topics below in the order listed.

  1. The problem (~1 slide). GHU-1’s failure, and how GHU-2’s design improves on GHU-1.

  2. Your design (~2 slides). The control approach for each actuator, your flowchart, and the one or two decisions you are most proud of. Show the result of your weighted decision matrix instead of the whole matrix.

  3. The evidence (~2 slides). Your acceptance-test results and the key performance plots: temperature through the storm, CO2 through the sensor freeze. Support each claim with the data.

  4. The humidity recommendation (~1 slide). What the client asked for, what the hardware allows, and what you recommend instead.

  5. What you learned (~1 slide). One thing about the problem and one about your team’s process.

20.4.2. Tips for a Successful Presentation#

  • Every team member speaks. Decide who covers what in advance.

  • Show data in place of descriptive words. A plot with a one-line takeaway is more convincing than a statement such as “it worked really well.”

  • Do not read your slides as a script. Use few words, clear figures, and text that is readable from the back of the room.

  • Practice the timing. Running over time is the most common mistake, and practice prevents it.

  • If your team presents in-person, be ready for questions. Mission Control might ask why you trusted the SCD41 over the K30, or how you chose your gains. Know your own numbers.

20.4.3. Submission#

Your team submits two items.

  1. Your slides, in the format your instructor specifies.

  2. Your live or recorded presentation, as your instructor directs.

Submit to 20.2.2 Milestone 5 - Project Presentation (Team) in Gradescope before the deadline on the Course Schedule.

Late submissions will NOT be accepted.

Learning Objectives

PC01:

Written Communication Standards - Use professional tone, clear sentence structure, and correct spelling and grammar in all written work.

PC03:

Captioning Visuals - Include professional captions for all figures, tables, and plots that describe what is shown and why it is relevant.

PC04:

Visual Communication of Engineering Contexts - Create visuals that clearly communicate engineering data, models, or concepts to a technical audience and support claims or conclusions.

SQ04:

Value Proposition - Present a clear value proposition highlighting benefits and novelty.

EC02:

Recognize Limits, Trade-offs, and Confidence - Recognize limitations, trade-offs, and uncertainty in engineering work and communicate appropriate levels of confidence in conclusions.
