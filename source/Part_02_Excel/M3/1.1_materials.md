# PCMs: Probability & Normal Distributions

These pre-class materials introduce statistical probability and distribution models that engineers use to evaluate reliability and predict outcomes based on data.

In engineering, understanding the likelihood of an event, such as a material failing at a certain stress level, is critical for design and safety.

## Relative Probability
**Relative probability** determines the likelihood of an event occurring based on collected data. The calculation method depends on whether the data represents a full population or a sample. 

When working with a specific dataset (a sample), you can determine the relative probability by comparing the number of times an event occurs to the total number of trials. Engineers often extract these frequency values directly from the bin counts of a histogram.

$$\text{Relative Probability} = \frac{\text{number of event occurrences}}{\text{total number of trials}}$$

### Example Calculations

#### Example 1: Drone Flights
If a histogram shows that an agricultural drone completed 50 total test flights (**total trials**), and 15 of those flights lasted longer than 20 minutes (**event occurrences**), the relative probability of a flight lasting longer than 20 minutes is calculated as:

$$\text{Relative Probability} = \frac{15}{50} = 0.30 \text{ (or 30%)}$$

## Normal Distributions
A **normal distribution**, often called a bell curve, is a specific way data can be distributed. The probability of an event is represented by a specific portion of the area under this curve.

A perfectly normal distribution has the following properties:
- The **mean, median, and mode** are all equal.
- The curve is perfectly **symmetric** at the center.
- The **total area** under the curve is exactly 1 (or 100%).

### Z-Scores
A **z-score** measures the distance of a specific data point from the dataset's mean, expressed in the number of standard deviations. If a dataset is normally distributed, engineers use z-scores to calculate the exact probability of an event occurring without needing to count individual bins on a histogram.

A z-score can be calculated from any data set using the expression: 

$$Z = \frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}$$ 

Where: 
- $x$ = data point
- $\mu$ = mean
- $\sigma$ = standard deviation

```{admonition} Information
:class: note
Z-scores can be efficiently calculated across an entire dataset using the expression above and cell references in Excel.
```


### Using Z-Tables
Z-tables help find the probability associated with a specific calculated z-score. Standard z-tables only display the **area to the left of the curve**. You may have to adjust your final calculation depending on the probability you are trying to find.

#### Example: Yellow Skittles
Imagine you calculate the exact statistics of your 150 Skittles bags. You find the mean ($\mu$) is 20% yellow Skittles, with a standard deviation ($\sigma$) of 6%. You want to find the exact probability of randomly selecting a bag with more than 27% yellow Skittles ($x$). 

First, calculate the z-score to find how many standard deviations 27% is from the mean.

$$Z = \frac{0.27-0.20}{0.06} = 1.16$$

From the z-table in {numref}`z-table`, the area to the left of a z-score of $1.16$ is $0.87698$. That means that there is an 87.698% probability that a random bag contains 27% or fewer yellow Skittles. 

However, you want the probability of randomly selecting a bag with **more** than 27% yellow Skittles, so you will subtract your value from 1. 

$$ \text{P}(\text{yellow} > 27\%) = 1 - 0.87698 = 0.123 \text{ or }12.3\%$$

```{admonition} Check for Understanding
:class: tip
An engineer is evaluating a new composite material and needs to find the probability of the tensile strength being 72 psi or greater. Using a standard z-table, they find the value corresponding to their calculated z-score is 0.3669. What is the correct next step to find the final probability?

A. Multiply 0.3669 by the standard deviation of the tensile strength.<br>
B. Use 0.3669 as the final probability, because the z-table directly provides the probability to the right.<br>
C. Subtract 0.3669 from 1, because the standard z-table only provides the probability to the left of the z-score.<br>
D. Subtract the z-score from 0.3669.

:::{dropdown} Show Answer
**Correct Answer: C** 

- You do not multiply the table probability value by the standard deviation.
- Standard z-tables provide the probability to the left of the curve, not the right.
- Subtracting the z-score from the probability value is not a valid statistical operation.
:::
```
