## Course Description
A partnership between Schools and Programs within the College of Engineering, 
this course introduces students to the engineering professions using 
multidisciplinary, societally relevant content. Students will be developing 
engineering approaches to systems, generating and exploring creative ideas, 
and using quantitative methods to support design decisions. Explicit 
model-development activities (engineering eliciting activities, EEAs) engage 
students in innovative thinking across the engineering disciplines at Purdue. 
Students will experience the process of design and analysis in engineering 
including how to work effectively in teams. Students will develop skills in 
project management, engineering fundamentals, oral and graphical communication, 
logical thinking, and modern engineering tools. 

## Learning Outcomes
In ENGR 131, you will learn to: 
-	Develop and evaluate mathematical models to describe or predict outcomes associated with engineering decisions.
-	Use a systematic design process to develop innovative solutions to engineering challenges, compare design alternatives, and gather and use evidence for decision making.
-	Examine and analyze information from various engineering schools at Purdue to make informed decisions about your choice of engineering major.
-	Practice making evidence-based engineering decisions. 
-	Work in diverse teams.
-	Develop professional habits including professional communication, teamwork, and information literacy that will benefit you both as a student at Purdue and as a practicing engineer.

## Assignments
ENGR 131 follows a flipped classroom model. Before class, you will review materials and complete activities related to course topics. In class, you will work on in-class activities, individually or in teams, related to the day’s topics. Assignments and activities that are traditionally considered as homework are started in class. The teaching team will be available during class time, so you can obtain help with understanding course topics and assignments from the various members of your teaching team.

In this course, the teaching team will assess your performance using the work you submit in four categories – pre-class assignments, individual assignments, team assignments, and exams. Each of these assessments will include detailed instructions on what work to do, how it will be evaluated, when it is due, and how to submit it. It is your responsibility to follow the instructions on individual assignments, complete the work on your own, and submit it on time. Similarly, each team member is responsible for contributing a fair share of the work on a team assignment and making sure it is submitted on time.

### Late Work Policy
On-time submission of your work is vital to successful performance in this course. Read each of your assignment’s instructions to see if late work is permitted.

```{rubric} Late work is only accepted as specified in the following cases: 
```

In these situations, arrange submission of your late work with your graduate teaching assistant (GTA) or instructor. 
-	**Submitting late individual work with a 25% penalty:** When late submissions are permitted, you may submit your work up to 24 hours late, but you will incur a deduction of 25% of the maximum possible points for the assignment.

## Attendance Policy
Maintaining regular contact with your instructor, classmates, and team is essential for success in this course. You are expected to attend all scheduled classes and actively participate in in-class activities. Your engagement benefits both your learning and your team’s progress.

### Absence-Related Professional Expectations
-	If you know you will miss a class, you must notify your instructor and GTA as soon as possible. Advance notice is expected except in emergencies. 
-	If you need an extension on an assignment due to an absence, a 24-hour extension is typical. However, unless the absence is excused by ODOS, extensions are **not automatic** and **must be requested in writing before the assignment deadline**.
-	Communication with your teammates is a professional courtesy.  This helps you stay informed about class topics and coordinate teamwork.
-	You are responsible for catching up on all information presented in class in preparation for the next class. 

### Exempt Absences
-	Absences excused by the Office of the Dean of Students (ODOS). Under academic regulations, excused absences may be granted by ODOS for cases of grief/bereavement, military service, jury duty, parenting leave, or emergent medical care. 
-	Chronic/long-term illness, or other emergencies with documentation. In rare cases, instructors may designate absences as exempt if there is valid documentation, but the situation is not covered by ODOS or by Disability Resource Center (DRC) accommodations.
-	Other situations may be excused at the discretion of your instructor, such as University-sanctioned athletics, bands, & orchestras.

### Non-Exempt Absences
- Each student is allowed **up to three non-exempt absences** during regular (non-exam) class periods without penalty. 
- These absences may occur for any reason (e.g., minor illness, travel, club/event participation).  
- See [Exam Period Absences](exam-absences) for make-up exam policies and restrictions.

### Exam Period Absences
- Students with an **exempt absence** during an exam period may complete a make-up exam.
- Students with a **non-exempt absence** during an exam period may complete a make-up exam only if:
  1. The absence was communicated in advance, and
  2. The absence was either due to an immediate need (e.g., illness) or was pre-approved in writing by the instructor (e.g., travel or activity participation).
- Students who miss an exam without any prior communication will receive a zero (0) on the exam.
- Make-up exams must be scheduled **outside regular class time**. It is the student’s responsibility to coordinate with the instructor to schedule the make-up exam **within one week** of the original exam date.

### Attendance Record Procedures
The teaching team will take attendance shortly after class starts. You will be marked **present** if you are seated with your team at the start of class. If you arrive late, you must quietly check in with the teaching team to ensure your attendance is recorded correctly.

You are responsible for regularly checking your attendance record and reporting any discrepancies to the teaching team promptly.

```{table} Attendance Record
:name: attendance-policy-table

|Category| Definition | Attendance Record | Attendance Count| 
| :-----: | :-----: | :-----: | :-----: | 
| Exempt | Did not attend class, with ODOS excused absence (or similar exemption) | E | -- |
```

Your non-exempt absence total is the sum of your attendance counts. You are allowed a non-exempt absence count up to three without penalty. Each additional non-exempt absence or tardy will result in in the following grade penalties:
- Each additional non-exempt absence (A) = 20-point deduction from overall grade
- Each additional tardy (T) = 10-point deduction from overall grade

If your non-exempt absence total **exceeds six (6)**, you will **fail the course**, regardless of your grade.

## Academic Integrity
You are a member of the Purdue community – a community that values integrity. Academic integrity is your pledge, as a member of the Purdue Engineering community, to uphold high standards of responsibility and honor in all your academic work. Your integrity is just as crucial to your engineering career as your technical knowledge. Academic dishonesty harms both. Build a strong foundation for ethical work and honorable standards as an engineer in your undergraduate years.

Your work in this class should fulfill Purdue’s student-created honor pledge:
> _As a Boilermaker pursuing academic excellence, I pledge to be honest and true in all that I do. Accountable together – We are Purdue.”_

To maintain academic integrity, comply with course and University policies. You will be held to the standards explained in these documents and to university regulations for student conduct.\
- [Statement of Integrity and Code of Conduct](https://www.purdue.edu/home/integrity_statement/)
- [Academic Integrity and You: Undergraduate Edition](https://www.purdue.edu/odos/osrr/academic-integrity/undergraduate.php)

You will be assessed in this course using individual and team submissions. When you work on individual assignments, your submissions must represent your unique work. For team assignments, all submissions must originate from the team, reflecting the team's collective effort. Originality in code not only demonstrates a student's understanding and mastery of the material but also ensures a fair and equitable assessment of their skills and knowledge. It serves as a fundamental measure of a student's ability to apply what they have learned in the course to solve specific problems. 

Submitted work will be periodically checked for various forms of academic dishonesty.

You are expected to submit solutions that are your own creation. You, individually or in a team, must design and develop your solutions without copying from or sharing with any outside sources (including, but not limited to, peers, electronic sharing sites, or generative AI tools). Failure to submit your own unique solutions undermines the learning process and violates the trust and expectations set by Purdue and the engineering community.

### Good Collaboration and Contributor Acknowledgement
You are encouraged to discuss course concepts and assignments with others in a way that enhances your learning. However, all work you submit must represent your own effort (for individual assignments) or your team's effort (for team assignments). If you receive substantial help from someone that directly influences your approach, design, or solution, you must list them as a contributor.

```{rubric} Examples of appropriate collaboration that require you to list a contributor. 
```
- A peer offers advice that influences the design of your code or your problem-solving approach.
- A classmate talks through a debugging strategy that helps you fix your work.
- You discuss different strategies for solving a problem and adapt an idea into your own solution.

```{rubric} Examples that do not require contributor acknowledgment: 
```
- Asking for clarification about assignment instructions or expectations.
- Discussing general course topics (e.g., Python syntax, how to use `countif`).
- Speaking with the instructional team or reviewing Python or Excel documentation.

You are expected to work independently on all individual assignments. Any work that appears to be substantially similar to another student’s submission may be investigated as academic dishonesty, even if both students cite each other.

### Generative AI Use in ENGR 131
Generative AI tools can be powerful aids in programming. You must use generative AI responsibly, in a manner that protects your ability to program and understand your own solutions. In this course, you may use generative AI tools, such as ChatGPT or Github Copilot, to help you learn about topics presented.

```{rubric} Permitted Uses
```
- Asking questions about general programming techniques or engineering concepts.
- Clarifying syntax or debugging logic in code you have already written.
- Developing personalized study materials or practice questions.
- Brainstorming or getting unstuck, after attempting the work on your own.


```{rubric} Prohibited Uses
```
- Submitting AI-generated code, text, or solutions as your own.
- Asking AI to complete or write solutions for any part of a graded assignment.
- Using AI to generate pseudocode, flowcharts, or detailed guidance on assignments.
- Using AI tools during exams or quizzes.
- Uploading any ENGR 131 course materials (assignments, prompts, slides, or exams) to an AI platform.

```{rubric} Documenting AI Use
```
All use of generative AI must be documented. When you use generative AI to aid your understanding, you must:
- Clearly state what tool you used.
- Describe how it contributed to your learning or solution.
- Explain any changes you made to the output.

You are responsible for verifying the accuracy of AI-generated content. Relying on generative AI without understanding its suggestions undermines your learning and may constitute academic dishonesty.

### Avoiding Self-Plagiarism
In this course, we uphold the highest standards of academic integrity, which includes avoiding self-plagiarism. Self-plagiarism occurs when a student reuses their own previously written work in a new context without proper citation. This can create a misleading impression of the student’s understanding and effort.

```{rubric} To avoid self-plagiarism, please adhere to the following guidelines: 
```
- **Original Work:** Ensure that all work submitted for this course is original and created specifically for each assignment. Reusing code or written work from previous assignments, semesters, or courses is not acceptable unless explicitly permitted by the instructor.
- **Transparency:** Be transparent with your instructor about your work. If you’re unsure whether reusing a piece of your own work might constitute self-plagiarism, ask your instructor for guidance.

Remember, the goal of each assignment is to enhance your learning and understanding. Each piece of work you submit should reflect your current knowledge and skills. Avoiding self-plagiarism ensures that you are always pushing forward in your learning journey.
