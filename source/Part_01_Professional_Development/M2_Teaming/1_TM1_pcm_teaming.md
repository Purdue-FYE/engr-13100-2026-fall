# PCM: Working in an Engineering Team
Engineering problems are often complex and require multiple perspectives. Because of this, engineers frequently work in **teams** to combine knowledge, skills, and ideas in order to achieve a shared goal.

## What is a Team?

A **team** is a formal work group consisting of people **working together to achieve a common goal**.

Teams are different from simple groups. In a group, people may work independently on similar tasks. In a team, members:

- Work **collaboratively toward a shared goal**
- Demonstrate **commitment to both the goal and to one another**

Successful teams depend on communication, shared responsibility, and mutual support.

## Benefits of Teams

Working in teams provides several important benefits.

### Creativity, Problem-Solving, and Innovation

When individuals from different backgrounds and perspectives work together, teams can develop:

- Creative approaches to solving problems
- Innovative ideas
- Higher-quality decisions

### Improved Communication

Effective teams share information and ideas regularly, which improves communication:

- Within the team
- Between the team and the broader organization

### Learning from Others

Team members bring different skills, experiences, and knowledge. Working together allows individuals to:

- Learn from one another
- Gain new perspectives

## Stages of Team Development

Most teams move through several stages as they learn to work together. Understanding these stages can help teams navigate challenges and become more effective.

### Forming

The team is first established and begins discussing the project.

Team members may:

- Feel tentative, polite, or optimistic
- Discuss objectives, scope, resources, and roles
- Make limited progress toward the goal at this stage

### Storming

Team members begin expressing opinions and concerns about the work and how it should be done.

Team members may:

- Feel less enthusiastic or resistant
- Experience conflicts about roles, responsibilities, or approaches
- Form subgroups
- Make slower progress temporarily

### Norming

The team develops shared expectations and begins working more cooperatively.

Team members may:

- Become more trusting and collaborative
- Feel comfortable sharing ideas
- Establish roles and team norms
- Experience less conflict and greater productivity

### Performing

The team works effectively toward achieving its goals.

Team members may:

- Feel motivated and productive
- Focus on shared objectives
- Handle conflicts constructively
- Anticipate and solve problems

### Adjourning

The team completes its work and disbands.

Team members may:

- Feel satisfaction with the team’s accomplishments
- Experience a sense of closure as the project ends

## Team Norms and Guidelines

Successful teams often establish a **team contract** or **code of cooperation**. These are shared guidelines that describe how team members will work together.

Examples of team guidelines include:

1. Attend all meetings and arrive on time.
2. Listen respectfully to the views of others.
3. Complete assigned work on time.
4. Resolve conflicts constructively.
5. Avoid disruptive side conversations.
6. Take responsibility for the team's progress.

Teams should periodically **review and update these guidelines** as needed.

## Running Effective Team Meetings

Productive teams use structured meetings to organize work and track progress. Several tools help teams stay focused and ensure tasks are completed.

### Agenda

An **agenda** is a plan for what will happen during a meeting. It outlines the topics the team will discuss and the order in which they will be addressed.

If the agenda changes unexpectedly:

- Meetings may need to be extended or rescheduled
- Some items may need to be postponed
- The team may fall behind schedule if important items are not addressed

### Action Items

**Action items** are a list of tasks that must be completed after a meeting.

Each action item should include:

- A **task name and description**
- The **team member responsible** for completing the task
- The **deadline** for completing the task

Clear action items help ensure that responsibilities are understood and progress continues between meetings.

### Issues Bin

An **issues bin** is a place to record topics that cannot be addressed during the current meeting.

Using an issues bin helps teams:

- Keep meetings focused on the agenda
- Ensure important topics are **not forgotten**
- Schedule time to address unresolved issues later

## Team Roles and Responsibilities

Teams often assign roles to help meetings run smoothly and ensure balanced participation. Leadership in teams should be **shared**, and these roles help organize responsibilities rather than create authority.

::::{grid} 1 1 2 2
:gutter: 3

:::{grid-item-card} Coordinator
:class-card: sd-shadow-sm

Organizes team activities and monitors the team’s progress.

Responsibilities may include:

- Planning meetings and activities
- Keeping meetings on track
- Monitoring team progress

The coordinator is not the boss of the team. This role helps organize communication and planning.
:::

:::{grid-item-card} Recorder
:class-card: sd-shadow-sm

Documents important information during team work sessions.

Responsibilities include:

- Writing meeting notes
- Recording decisions and action items
- Sharing meeting summaries with the team

The recorder should ensure that all team members understand the information recorded.
:::

:::{grid-item-card} Timekeeper
:class-card: sd-shadow-sm

Monitors how time is being used during meetings.

Responsibilities include:

- Notifying the team if discussions are running long
- Helping the team stay focused on the current task

The timekeeper does not control the schedule but alerts the team when adjustments may be needed.
:::

:::{grid-item-card} Encourager / Gatekeeper
:class-card: sd-shadow-sm

Helps maintain balanced participation among team members.

Responsibilities include:

- Encouraging quieter members to contribute ideas
- Preventing one member from dominating discussions
- Promoting inclusive participation
:::

:::{grid-item-card} Devil’s Advocate
:class-card: sd-shadow-sm
:columns: 12

Raises alternative viewpoints to challenge assumptions.

This role helps teams:

- Avoid rushing to decisions
- Consider additional perspectives
- Evaluate ideas more critically

This role is often temporary and used when a team may be rushing toward a decision.
:::

::::

## Teaming Best Practices

Effective teams often demonstrate several key behaviors.

::::{grid} 1 1 2 2
:gutter: 3

:::{grid-item-card} Actively Participate
:class-card: sd-shadow-sm

Team members contribute ideas, ask questions, and stay engaged in discussions.

Active participation helps ensure that all perspectives are considered.
:::

:::{grid-item-card} Handle Conflict Directly
:class-card: sd-shadow-sm

Disagreements are common in teamwork.

Addressing conflicts respectfully and constructively helps teams resolve issues and continue making progress.
:::

:::{grid-item-card} Communicate Openly and Positively
:class-card: sd-shadow-sm

Clear communication helps ensure that everyone understands goals, expectations, and responsibilities.
:::

:::{grid-item-card} Be Reliable

## Dealing With Team Issues

Sometimes teams encounter challenges that cannot be resolved easily within the group.

If your team is struggling to work productively together:

1. Try discussing the issue respectfully with your teammates.
2. Clarify expectations and responsibilities.
3. Use structured tools such as **action items** to track responsibilities.

If problems continue, speak with your **GTA or instructor**. They are available to help support your team.
