Given: the indicator response times are approximately normally distributed with:
- Mean: $\mu = 10$ seconds
- Variance: 4 seconds $^2$
- Critical points: 7 and 12 (between)

We know that standard deviation is the square root of variance, so
- Standard Deviation $\sigma$ = 2 seconds

To compute the Z-score, we use the formula

$Z = \dfrac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$,

where:
- $x$ is the observed value,
- $\mu$ is the mean,
- $\sigma$ is the standard deviation.

Critical value 7:

$Z_1 = \frac{7 - 10}{2} = -1.5$

Critical value 12:

$Z_2 = \frac{12 - 10}{2} = 1$

From the Z-table:

$P(Z \le -1.5) = 0.0668$

$P(Z \le 1) = 0.8413$

We want to know the area between these two, so we subtract lower from upper

$P(-1.5 \le Z \le 1) = 0.8412 - 0.0668 = 0.7745$

Converting to a percentage:

0.7745 x 100% = 77.5%

Therefore, approximately **$77.5\$ %** of the trials are moderately controlled. #
