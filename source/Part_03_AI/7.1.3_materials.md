# PCMs: What is Artificial Intelligence? 

These pre-class materials introduce the foundational concepts of artificial intelligence (AI) and how mathematical models simulate intelligent behavior.

In engineering, you must understand how AI tools generate their outputs so you can critically evaluate their predictions. In this module, you will learn the difference between simulated and actual intelligence, the definition of modern AI, and the primary categories of AI models.

## Defining Artificial Intelligence
The term artificial intelligence can refer to two different concepts:
* **Strong AI:** A theoretical system that possesses an actual mind, consciousness, and a true understanding of other individuals. The AI itself is not simulating intelligence; it is actually intelligent. 
* **Weak (Narrow) AI:** A system that simulates intelligent behavior but does not possess actual understanding, consciousness, or mental states. Although it appears intelligent, there is no claim that the system understands what it is doing. 

Current AI technology is strictly weak AI. A computer executing a program to translate symbols may appear intelligent to an outside observer, but the system itself has no actual comprehension of the task. Modern artificial intelligence is defined as a system that can perform tasks requiring human intelligence while learning and adapting to changing contexts.

### How Modern AI Operates
At its core, modern artificial intelligence reduces to making predictions using mathematical and algorithmic models. Rather than thinking or reasoning, these systems calculate probabilities, categorize data, output numeric predictions, or generate sequences.

Engineers categorize these systems based on how they process information and generate outputs.

## Types of AI Models

| AI Model Category | How It Operates | Common Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Rule & Logic-Based | Uses predetermined rules and expert databases to dictate behavior. Does not learn from new data. | Autonomous movement logic for non-playable characters in video games. |
| Machine Learning: Prediction | Learns directly from data to calculate specific numerical outcomes. | Linear regression, tree-based models (decision trees, random forests), Bayesian probabilistic models, and neural networks. |
| Machine Learning: Discriminative | Learns directly from data to categorize or group information into distinct sets. | k-means clustering, principal components analysis (PCA), and neural networks. |
| Machine Learning: Generative | Learns directly from data to create entirely new content (text, image, video) based on prompts. | Transformer-based LLMs (ChatGPT, Google Gemini, DeepSeek), Diffusion models (Veo, Sora), and General Adversarial Networks. |

## Engineering Implications
Because AI is ubiquitous in modern engineering, you must understand how these underlying mathematical models work. Human intelligence involves understanding, reasoning, and consciousness. Artificial intelligence uses math and algorithms to simulate the product an intelligent human would create. Because an AI system contains no actual understanding, engineers must independently verify the accuracy and safety of its predictions.
